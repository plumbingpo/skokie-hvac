How Chicago Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your system. Its age tells you a lot. Most well-maintained air conditioners make it to 12 to 15 years, gas furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that range and also shows rising bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or frequent repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement when the first heat wave hits.

Small problems don't stay minor when they're ignored. Consider a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a quick, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and just like that a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a few miserable nights.
